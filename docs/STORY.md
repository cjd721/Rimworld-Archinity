# The story

The whole-book layer. Everything here is true across all six eras and none of it belongs
to one of them. Plot lines and acts read this and take their material from it; nothing
below is era-specific, and anything that becomes era-specific has stopped belonging here.

**Standing.** This document is the source of truth for the fiction from the day Conrad
approves it, and not before. Until then `docs/STORY-CANON.md` remains the working
compilation. On approval, `STORY-CANON.md` and `cosmology-in-practice.md` are archived,
`COSMOLOGY.md` survives only for mechanism this document does not carry and is demoted to
downstream of it, and `WAYSTONE.md` is untouched and continues to govern the design.
**That archival happens on Conrad's approval only.**

## The premise

The Archons built this universe and they built it as a test. They are not gods in the
sense of having created everything — they are a higher order that authors lower ones, and
what they are farming for is potential. They seed a dimension, they leave, and they watch
whether anything inside can climb out under its own power. A thing that arrives at the top
carried is not a thing that climbed, which is why they granted nothing to anybody and why
the tower has to be walked floor by floor. The eras are the floors. Neolithic, Medieval,
Industrial, Spacer, Ultra — and then a door.

Beyond this reality is the transcendent. True power, in the sense the word carries in the
stories this campaign is descended from: not more force, but the thing this whole universe
is a room inside of. It is a place and it presses through. Some people are sensitive enough
to feel it without knowing what they are feeling. Some train for a lifetime and win a
sliver of access. Some study the universe's inconsistencies and trail the seams back toward
it. Every civilisation in this story that has ever amounted to anything is doing one of
those three.

**The way up is the narrow road, and it is open to everyone.** There is no gate on it and
there never was. It goes unwalked because it is brutal — it asks for every burden, every
trial, and the proper sacrifices — and because losing yourself along the way is the default
outcome rather than the exception. Some in this universe have probably made it. Traces of
them probably survive. Nothing will ever confirm it.

**The road is personal.** It is not one path that everyone must find; it is each thing's
own highest path. A hero who climbed on good deeds and a conqueror who climbed on conquest
have both walked theirs, and both arrive. The test is not which road you took. It is
whether you were still walking it at the end.

**The founders were not given access.** Access was never restricted. They were given a
shove and a pointed finger — the mark is an invitation to be measured, nothing more. The
Archons intervene only when the experiment stalls, and the first and largest intervention
was waking a world that had sat at the Neolithic for a very long time. Everything since has
been a probe.

### The two roads

**The path of power and the right path run parallel.** They travel the same direction by
necessity — you cannot walk the narrow road without becoming enormously powerful, and every
rung of the campaign hands over more. But they are not the same road and they do not fail
the same way.

**The path of power tops out.** It converges with the right path at the very end and then
the right path keeps going, and that last stretch is not made of power at all.

What ends a climb is losing your humanity. **The Church lost itself to greed. The
Glitterites lost themselves to efficiency.** Both hold more power than the founders ever
will. Neither will ascend. Neither can see it — each is staring directly at the half of
itself it amputated, and each has enough power that nothing in this universe is in a
position to say so.

That is the warning this campaign carries, and it is not *power corrupts*. It is that a
thing can keep every capability it ever had, and stop.

### What is being measured

Not strength. Strength is common — the Glitterites hold galaxies of it and are going
nowhere. What is measured is whether a thing can take on that much power without losing
hold of what the power was ever for.

**Intent is assumed, never measured.** There is no purity meter, no hidden score, no moral
ledger, and there never will be — anything tracked becomes a number, anything numbered gets
optimised, and a thing that can be optimised was never the thing. The game systems compel
the doing: you cannot grow in power without converting people and raising their conviction,
and you cannot reach the altar's gifts without making the sacrifices. The main questline
supplies the direction. The player supplies the reasons, and the campaign takes them on
faith.

**Transcendence is graduation, not escape.** You become more than this reality can hold,
you see its rules for the illusions they always were, and you step through. The burdens do
not end because you outlasted them. They end because you transcended them.

## Tone and register

**Sacral and gothic with an edge of realism.** Roman Catholic Church meets Vlad the
Impaler. Cosmic horror is refused outright and the refusal does not reopen: the Archons'
otherness is **sacral, not void** — gods, not entities. The medieval-creature material is
in for *demonic* flavour, never eldritch. Vampires are the same phenomenon further down the
line, an inheritance of the same mark thinned at every hand it passed through; the vampire
reading is leaned into rather than softened.

Sources, recorded so the register can be recovered: **donghua** — the interdimensional
power struggle, power cultivated from a realm beyond this one; **manhwa** — the tower of
trials, higher beings who gift power, test it, and pull the cream into their own
machinations; the **Book of Enoch**; and **The Matrix**.

**The invitation sets the tone for the whole campaign.** First contact is a measurement,
delivered with the indifference of something enormous that has done this many times before:
*here is the bar. You can join us. You are not nearly strong enough.* Not a briefing, not a
bargain, not an explanation. The bar is shown on day one and never once explained.

### The rules that govern every line of copy

**Nothing is ever stated plainly.** No letter, no quest description, no research blurb
explains the tower, the test, or the Archons' purpose. The moment the fiction confirms its
own premise, the best thing about it dies. Write toward a grander scheme and never name it.

**Hand over the facts. Never hand over the meaning.** The founders' faction knows what
happened to them by testimony rather than by faith, and that testimony narrows the field
without closing it.

**It is testimony, not consensus.** Nobody else on the planet is obliged to believe it and
mostly they do not. The Church's account of how the world began has no Archon in it. A world
where every religion is a translation of one memo is a world with one religion in it.

**Never adjudicate between faiths.** The campaign knows which account is factually correct
and must never once say so or reward it. Being right about the facts is not the
qualification. Knowing what happened is not the qualification — what you made of it is.

**How it is told evolves; what is told does not.** The form the account takes changes every
time the world around it does — spoken, then codified, then argued over by people who no
longer agree on the translation, then read back much later by someone who finds it was
accurate the whole time. The content underneath never moves an inch. A player watching one
truth survive four retellings is doing more work than any exposition could. **What each
era's telling actually looks like belongs to the plot-line layer, not to this document.**

**Write the gap, never fill it.** Refer to *what your people hold true*, to conviction, to
the thing the founders reach through — second person and abstract, so it lands on whatever
the player actually chose. That phrasing is the whole technique. The game does not check
your answer; it checks that you answered.

**One strange noun, permanently: `anima`.** Everything else is plain English — blood, the
mark, the climb, the price, worship, sacrifice, taken, given, tainted, pure, channel, clear,
heart. A world with one strange word is mystical; a world with five is a rulebook. Avoid
industrial metaphors for anima entirely: it is not ore, not material, not fuel. Describe the
channel and never specify it — no mechanism, no obstruction list, no rating, no diagram, and
no name for the condition, because a name makes it a category and a category invites a stat.

**Written for a player who takes it seriously**, and that assumption is allowed. Designing
around someone clicking through ideology setup at random would mean scoring sincerity, and
sincerity is the one thing that cannot be scored.

## The arc

The spine runs dawn to door and **it only goes up.** Neolithic to orbit to the threshold,
every era in order, each one handing over more than the last. The era-by-era shape — what
the founders gain and what the world does back — belongs to the plot-line layer and is not
restated here.

### There is no floor, and that is deliberate

This campaign does not author its own low points and must not try.

RimWorld is a story generator with a storyteller sitting on top of it, and the highs and
lows belong to it. A quest sends a caravan out; the storyteller throws a raid at the colony
while it is gone; the colony is gutted; the caravan comes home with the power anyway and
that power goes into a founder at the cost of the last few colonists; and then the founders
go out and take fifteen more people by force. Nobody wrote that, and nobody should try to.
**We author the direction, the world, the options, and what the five senses report.
Everything that happens to the player is generated at play time.**

So the authored plot arc ascends without interruption, and this is not a story without
tension — it is a story whose tension is supplied by a better instrument than an outline.
The game is hard, and should be harder than an ordinary hard playthrough, because it starts
with two demigods. A founder can be killed permanently in the first week by a raid that
happened to land right. You should lose people, not runs — and sometimes you will lose more
than that.

**Any search for a floor, a low point, or a dark-night-of-the-soul in this campaign is a
category error and should be recognised as one.** It is a question about an authored
narrative, and this is not one.

### Nothing pushes

There is no clock, no countdown, no rival racing you, and no consequence for taking your
time. The world advances only when the founders advance it: the era rite fires and the world
visibly ages in a moment. The storyteller scales its pressure to the colony's power rather
than to the calendar.

**Stopping is a legitimate campaign.** A player who parks in the Medieval for a thousand
hours and builds the largest colony anyone has ever seen, and then moves on when they are
finally bored, has played this correctly. The main plot is always available and never
urgent.

### What carries the passage of time

RimWorld's years pass quickly and flatly, and nobody actually crosses from the Neolithic to
the Industrial in a lifetime. The compression is a concession to the fact that a human
cannot play ten thousand hours — **it is not a story element and must never be written as
one.**

Elapsed time is carried by the fiction instead, and chiefly by two instruments: **the prose**,
which says how long things have taken, and **succession** — the leaders of the world's
factions age, die, and are replaced across the campaign while the founders do not. The cast
is a set of **roles**, not names. *The leader of the Church* is the correct resolution at
this layer; who he turns out to be is discovered when the circumstances that pin him down
have actually happened, and those circumstances will tell a better story than anything
decided in advance.

### The founders at the opening

They begin deathless and impossibly strong, and skilled at nothing. That combination is the
whole of the opening: against a world of sticks and hide they are unmistakably divine, and
they cannot yet do anything well. **The strength is a problem as often as a solution** —
they have no fine control of it, and the world has to be handled around that fact. Everything
they are good at, they earn.

### How often the Archons appear

**Three times in the whole campaign, and only two of them are witnessed.** The marking
itself is the first — prehistoric, wordless, and never seen in play. The invitation is the
second, and it is the campaign's opening contact. The door at the end is the third.

**No act adds a fourth, and this is a hard budget rather than a guideline.** An Archon who
turns up whenever a plot line needs weight is not a higher order; it is a recurring
character. Their scarcity is the only reason a single appearance carries anything at all.

### The ending

They arrive at the door and it opens. Stepping through is a victory condition **and it can
be declined**: step through and roll credits, or step through, look, and come back to keep
playing as a god in a universe that can still kill everyone but them. The offer stays
standing.

**The other side is never shown.** The whole campaign runs on one sentence — *you are not
nearly strong enough* — and the ending is the moment that sentence stops being true. Showing
what is past the threshold would be the largest breach of the curtain in the project.

### The co-op frame

The campaign is designed, built and playtested as **single-player**, and migrated to
Multiplayer afterward by patch. **There are no narrative changes for co-op.** Nothing in this
document, or anything downstream of it, is written for two players.

## What the world lets them work out

The player is meant to assemble a private theory of what this universe is, and to remain
permanently unsure whether they got it right. Two players arguing about it at three in the
morning, both wrong in different ways, is the target state.

### Where the curtain is

**The curtain sits at *why*.**

Everything about **what** happens and **how** the world works is on the near side and should
be painted generously. Tease it, hook them with it, build mystery out of it — the method of
revelation on this side is where the writing gets to have all of its fun. That the Archons
exist and marked this line by hand; that there is a bar and something past it; that anima is
the life of the universe and behaves consistently; that blood taken differs from blood given;
that other powers are climbing and want things; that the Church's congregation is sincere and
its leadership is not; that something enormous is in orbit and will not open the door.

Every question of **intent, purpose, or the nature of the thing running this** stays behind
it, permanently.

### How the near side is revealed

**Only ever as things that should not be true, and are.** Never as a vision, a voice, a
glimpse, or a revelation scene.

And the loudest of them is the player's own faction. People who do not die. People who grow
strong as gods. Reaching past this reality and pulling something back through it. The feeling
of being watched constantly and being completely alone at the same time. **The channel is the
ultimate anomaly, and the anomaly is you.**

### What is never confirmed

Named here so that no later ticket closes one of them in passing. Every line of copy should
make these *reachable* and no line may ever land on one.

- **Whether this reality is real.** Permanently open, by design.
- **What the Archons actually are**, why they built this, and whether they are benevolent,
  indifferent, or entertained.
- **Whether the Archons are graduates of this same road.** Some of them probably are. There
  may well be ranks among them — the lower orders spinning up universes and farming talent
  while the upper echelon has gone past needing to. It is the most satisfying conclusion a
  player can reach unaided and the most damaging thing the game could ever state.
- **Whether anyone else has made it, and what the traces mean.** Probably yes and probably
  they survive. Nothing ever resolves whether a given trace belongs to someone who got out or
  someone who died on the way. Good material for subplots and side quests.
- **How many were marked.** Held open on purpose. The Archons are fickle and do as they
  please, frequently in secret.

# Neolithic

[Campaign overview](../PLOT.md) · Next: [Medieval](MEDIEVAL.md)

## Prologue & Neolithic Arc

## Prologue — The Mark, the First Thought and the Waystone

The Archon intervention is not a technology lesson. The mark represents a qualitative change in capacity: abstraction, planning, imagining circumstances beyond the immediate present, tool-making and cumulative civilization. Think of it as the revelation of modern consciousness, not the Archon handing cavemen a farming manual.

The founders begin extraordinarily resilient and deathless, but skilled at nothing. The Archon also leaves the Waystone, a tiny unfathomable piece of Archotech that reacts to other Archotech. The founders still have to invent civilization and learn how to interpret it. No mood debuff forces the altar hunt; the Star Table provides the voluntary player-facing hook.

## Neolithic I — Awakening

Establish the first verbs of civilization: fire, shelter, hunting, crude tools, food security and basic organization.

Build the Star Table. A pawn combines ordinary surveying with repeated observations of the Waystone until an Archon return can be located.

The first core discovery leads to the altar or otherwise establishes it as the central strange artifact of the campaign.

The altar accepts blood/life and visibly holds a charge. Nothing transformative happens yet. The intended reaction is not “the sacrifice did nothing,” but “the machine accepted it and I am missing another condition.”

## The Anima-Tree People — Optional Early Worldbuilding

The anima-tree faction is optional and behaves like an ambient threshold encounter, not a mandatory chain. They can invite the colony to visit, trade and learn; the player can ignore them without losing the campaign.

They teach that anima exists, living things carry it, blood releases/concentrates it, and anima trees interact with it naturally.

They explain the founders’ blood hunger and the altar’s charge without explaining the full Archon cosmology.

They do not grant the first psylink and do not own the campaign-critical path.

## Neolithic II — Primitive Baseline

Permanent settlement begins: farming, production tables, stone construction, leather armor, cloth clothing and reliable infrastructure.

The founders receive the Genius Archogene around this stage. The meaning is “they can now become good at things,” not “they woke up already competent.”

Charting produces more side locations, but rewards remain immediately useful to primitive civilization. No repeatable capsule economy yet.

## Neolithic III — Settled Tribe

The local map becomes solved: defensible settlement, food security, walls, organized labor, trained fighters and a recognizable culture.

No psychic progression axis is added here. The player finishes learning primitive civilization before organized religion arrives.

The era closes as the colony masters its immediate world and the wider political world becomes impossible to ignore.

**Neolithic endpoint**

The player knows the founders are biologically strange, blood contains anima, the altar stores a charge, the Waystone can lead toward Archotech traces, and civilization extends beyond the home tile. They do not yet know what willing devotion does, and they have no psylink.

The opening contact is the invitation; the prehistoric marking is not witnessed
in play. The founders' strength initially exceeds their fine control. Injury can
cost limbs and months of usefulness.

**The founders can die, and the campaign can end.** Deathlessness removes the
ordinary causes and rapid regrowth and accelerated healing carry them through
injuries that would kill anyone else, but destruction of the brain kills them
permanently. That holds from the prologue until the altar authors the Transcendent
Archogene, after which death becomes temporary absence. The resilience is genetic,
not narrative armor: it is what makes losing a founder to one bad afternoon
unlikely rather than what makes it impossible. Hard to kill, not unkillable.

The gene package that delivers this is [#31](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/31)'s
to select, and the deathless-plus-brain-destruction behavior needs verifying
against 1.6 rather than assuming; the post-transcendence gene is described in
[the ending](ENDING.md#the-transcendent-archogene).

Discovery mechanics are in [Charting](../specs/CHARTING.md); founder rewards and
the altar's separate progression tracks are in [the altar specification](../specs/ALTAR.md).

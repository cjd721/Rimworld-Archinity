# Medieval

Previous: [Neolithic](NEOLITHIC.md) · [Overview](../PLOT.md) · Next: [Industrial](INDUSTRIAL.md)

## Medieval Arc — Seduction, Reverence and Schism

Medieval is the first fully authored political act. Organized civilization notices the founders. The dominant Church recognizes them as sacred proof, teaches them something real, offers extraordinary rewards, uses their notoriety to grow its own power and eventually fears the possibility that worship will bypass the institution and flow directly into the founders’ ideology.

The Church's Exaltation/title ladder and the Schism's spendable Influence are
defined in [religion and politics](../requirements/RELIGION.md). They coexist with
Goodwill, Reverence and the independent psychic ladder.

## Medieval I — The Forge / Seduction

Metalworking transforms the colony. Expert Social remains a strong candidate for the first Medieval founder vector because the new bottleneck is people, conversion, diplomacy and scale rather than raw survival.

The first major public deed that introduces Reverence should feel heroic and consequential—a faction asks for help against a looming threat, the founders intervene, and the world begins telling stories about what happened.

The Church courts the founders and delivers the first willing-blood rite / psylink breakthrough.

The player begins seeing Goodwill and Reverence side-by-side in the political UI and can immediately see future diplomatic/religious actions gated behind higher Reverence.

## Medieval II — Tension

The Church relationship is systemic rather than a fixed “quest six betrays you” chain. As long as the founders remain useful and the Church can plausibly contain their independent religious growth, it continues offering title quests. A player who continually lets the Church absorb credit can climb much farther. A player whose own ideology spreads rapidly may become dangerous after only one or two titles.

Other Medieval factions react to era, research, Goodwill, Church title, Reverence and prior choices; they can ally, feud and go to war without each needing a bespoke linear questline.

Roads become political infrastructure. Medieval powers maintain serious routes, contest trade corridors and can request or finance road construction. The world begins visibly connecting itself.

Reverence starts producing actual religious movement: converted visitors return as apostles, pilgrims arrive, and factions with growing faith become places where permanent religious institutions may later be established.

## The Schism — Believers Inside the Church

The anti-Church faction forms from inside the Church because it believed the Church’s own claims about the founders. Senior clergy, militant orders and followers were taught that the founders are sacred proof. They witness the founders, believe the doctrine, and then watch leadership prepare to contain or destroy the beings it told them to revere. That contradiction creates the break.

The faction name remains open. “Witnesses” is a discarded placeholder, not final canon.

The schism can include one or two high-ranking officials plus their subordinate network, giving it immediate institutional knowledge and operational reach.

They do not know the hidden Archon cosmology. Their faith can be sincere and wrong in details while still correctly identifying the Church’s hypocrisy.

Their proposition is personal: “They are using you. We believed what they taught us about you, and now we know what they intend to do.”

The contact point is threshold-driven. If the founders’ direct Reverence becomes dangerous to Church control, the schism offers warning and help before the Church moves. Accepting breaks on favorable terms. Rejecting does not permanently preserve the status quo: if the threat threshold is already crossed, the Church can still initiate betrayal later.

## Medieval III — Schism / War

Political pressure becomes open conflict: specialized armies, sieges, demands to choose sides, faction wars and large-scale obligations.

Church route: remain a sanctioned sacred asset, keep climbing the Exaltation/title ladder and accept increasingly explicit institutional ownership in exchange for exceptional power.

Schism route: use Influence, Reverence and military force to break the old hierarchy. The locked invariant is that if this route succeeds, the old hierarchy falls and the founder-believing breakaway inherits enough people, infrastructure and organization to become the successor religious institution. Its exact autonomy/vassal status remains a player outcome.

Independent route: reject both institutions and build authority through the player’s ideology, Reverence, Goodwill, vassalage, diplomacy and conquest.

**Three valid planetary religious outcomes**

1) Sanctioned Divinity — remain inside the Church. 2) Schism Successor — overthrow the old hierarchy and carry forward the founder-believing successor institution. 3) Independent Divinity — reject both and build a founder-centered religious/political network directly.

The final Schism name, Church title names and exact public-deed scenes remain to
be authored. Successful Schism succession is fixed; its autonomy or subordination
is the player's outcome. The Church route's later faith commitment is described
in [the ending](ENDING.md#the-alignment-rule--faith-cannot-be-borrowed-dishonestly).

# Map #2 — structural changes log, to 2026-09-16

History, not authority. This is the *Structural changes* section of
[map #2](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/2) as it stood when the map was split
on 2026-09-16 into a capability map (#2) and a build map
([#119](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/119)). It records how the map changed
shape — the layer gates, the batch close-outs, the tooling defects — and moved here because the
map is an index, not a store. Stage references to beats, grids and sourcing now belong to #119.

## The map's frame before the split

### The workflow

Five stages. They are an order of *derivation*, not a set of gates: a ticket waits on the
tickets it actually needs and on nothing else.

| Stage | The question it answers | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Campaign arc** | *What is this campaign, and what is it meant to feel like?* Its major developments and intended experience. | `docs/PLOT.md` and the era chapters in `docs/plot/` — **settled** on [the main plot arc](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/11) |
| **2 · System requirements** | *What must the game support?* Derived from the arc. Meaning, required behavior, progression, player agency, constraints. Where the arc leaves a requirement incomplete, the owning design ticket fills it in. | `docs/requirements/` |
| **3 · Capability research and technical specs** | *Can we actually do that in RimWorld 1.6, and at what cost?* Vanilla, DLC and mods — does the mechanism exist, what carries it, XML or C#, is it multiplayer-safe, what does it constrain. | `docs/specs/`, and cross-cutting facts in `docs/TRAPS.md` and `docs/engine/` |
| **4 · Authored plot beats** | *What happens, exactly?* Every quest, encounter and scripted event, written inside the established arc and the verified capabilities. | The era chapters in `docs/plot/`, filled |
| **5 · Progression grids, then sourcing** | *What does the player have and when — and where does each row come from?* | `docs/progression/`, then the sourcing ledger and the implementation items |

**Stage 5's per-domain sourcing passes do not exist yet, deliberately.** The grids decide
how many there are and what they cover; creating them now would be charting fog.
[The sourcing ledger](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/14) builds the artifact they write into, and it waits on the
grids for the same reason — its row set is a superset of theirs and its rows are keyed by
their names. **Stage 5 is genuinely last**, and the ledger is the last thing in it.

### The rule that replaced the layer gates

> Replace blanket layer gates with explicit dependencies. Derive system requirements from
> the established campaign arc, verify their technical feasibility and constraints, then
> author individual events within those capabilities. Requirements work may precede
> capability research; individual beat authoring must not be used as its prerequisite. A
> capability investigation may proceed when its own requirements are ready, independently
> of unrelated systems.

Dependencies follow the **relevant system**. A Charting investigation proceeds once
Charting's requirements are sufficient; it does not wait on unrelated religion
requirements. An encounter that uses two systems waits for the relevant answers from both.

The crucial distinction, and the one the old `layer:narrative` label hid:

- **System design** — *"What must religious propagation do?"* Precedes and unblocks
  capability research.
- **Beat authoring** — *"What happens during this particular pilgrimage encounter?"* Uses
  the capability research and comes afterward.

A ticket that carries both gets split, so it cannot create a circular dependency.

### The three artifacts

| Artifact | What it owns | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Requirements** (`docs/requirements/`) | Intended experience, meaning, behavior and constraints | Reverence represents religious adoption, gates options, and remains separate from Goodwill. |
| **Technical specification** (`docs/specs/`) | How the system will satisfy those requirements | State ownership, conversion hooks, persistence, synchronization, UI integration, existing machinery and required new code. |
| **Narrative chapter** (`docs/plot/`) | The particular events through which the player encounters it | A public deed introduces Reverence; the Church later fears the founders' independent following. |

**A capability is something the game must be able to do**, defined by observable behavior,
independently of its implementation — *maintain a persistent measure of religious adoption
per faction*; *discover the next eligible campaign site through pawn labor*; *let a hacker
at home act through a deployed pawn*. A mod is a possible *provider* of capabilities. A
requirement states the conditions a capability must satisfy.

**Specs are organized by system, not by mod and not by ticket.** Several capability tickets
feed one religion spec. A substantial subsystem earns its own document when the content
warrants it; do not manufacture a document for every hook someone investigates.

**A research result has three destinations.** The issue keeps the investigation, the
evidence and the resolution. The system's spec keeps the supported mechanism, its
constraints, the unresolved questions and the resulting design. `docs/TRAPS.md` and `docs/engine/`
keep only what is worth knowing outside that system, with a link rather than a copy. And
**verified is not selected**: an investigation can establish that a mechanism exists
without settling the design — say *"verified available mechanism"*, and let it become an
implementation commitment only when that choice is made.

The rules live beside the documents they govern — `docs/requirements/README.md` and
`docs/specs/README.md` carry a template each; `docs/progression/README.md` carries the
grid's shape and boundaries.

## Structural changes

Recorded because they change the shape of the map rather than its route.

- **The three layer gates are closed** — [#63](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/63), [#64](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/64), [#65](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/65). Replaced by explicit, system-scoped edges.

[The pre-worldgen freeze](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/18) moved the other way, to `layer:sourcing`: it asks nothing, consumes other tickets' answers, and produces the checklist implementation executes.

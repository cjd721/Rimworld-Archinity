# World infrastructure

Roads express the world's technological era and political relationships on the world map.
They are not produced by Charting and are not an altar reward.

## Era-driven network growth

Completing an era capstone advances the road quality available to civilizations. After
each advance, every civilization begins building or upgrading routes between its own
settlements and the settlements of allied neighboring factions. This is a world-wide
process, not a radius around the player and not a one-time player-faction effect.

Construction should unfold visibly over time—roughly 30 in-game days as a starting
target—rather than replacing the map instantly. The exact duration, eligible neighbor
definition and route-selection priorities are balance and capability questions.

## Player agency

The player should be able to contribute resources to a planned route to complete it sooner
or extend it farther than the autonomous network would. The player should also be able to
build roads directly. Those are complementary verbs: funding participates in civilization
projects; direct construction lets the colony create its own route.

## Boundaries and remaining work

Road tier, route ownership, alliances and actual constructed edges are world
infrastructure state. Charting may later read completed mobility as one input to reach, but
it does not own road creation, timing or cost.

Capability research must settle how runtime road edges are scheduled, upgraded and drawn;
whether partial construction can be represented; how player contributions alter time or
distance; how direct building works; and how all writes remain deterministic in
Multiplayer. [#68](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/68)
owns that investigation.

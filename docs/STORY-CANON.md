# The story canon

Everything the campaign has settled about its own story, in one place.

**Why this exists.** The narrative was scattered across five documents and the
resolution comments of five closed tickets, and no session had ever read all of it
at once. Every story ticket downstream would otherwise have begun by re-gathering
the same material and quietly disagreeing with the last session about what was
settled. This is that gather, done once.
[The story canon](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/36).

**Nothing here is new.** This document invents no story. It consolidates, marks,
and — where two sources disagree — records both readings without picking a winner.
The disagreements are §12, and resolving them is the work of the tickets
downstream, not of this file.

---

## How to read it

Every claim carries a mark:

| Mark | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **[SETTLED]** | Carried in a closed ticket's resolution, or a line in a live document that a closed ticket ratifies or amends. Safe to write against. |
| **[DRAFT]** | Written down, never ratified — mostly `docs/archive/`. True until somebody says otherwise, and not safe to build a beat on. |
| **[OPEN]** | Deliberately unresolved. Named as open by Conrad or by a live document. Do not close it in passing. |

A ⚠ points at **§12 Contradictions on the table**.

**Where a claim comes from**, and which source wins when two touch — this
precedence is recorded in the documents themselves, not invented here:

- A **closed ticket's resolution comment** is the decision. It is the top of the stack.
- **`docs/WAYSTONE.md`** governs the design. It is live and is edited when a ticket changes it.
- **`docs/COSMOLOGY.md`** governs the fiction. *"Where the two touch, Waystone governs the design and this governs the fiction."*
- **`docs/cosmology-in-practice.md`** is downstream of COSMOLOGY and cites rather than restates it. ⚠ Its standing was downgraded — see §12.7.
- **`docs/archive/`** — `VISION.md`, `QUESTLINE.md` — is mined for intent, never cited as authority. Distrust anything in it that depends on a particular mod set.
- **`CONTEXT.md`** is the glossary. It names things; it does not decide them.

This document does not restate the glossary and does not carry mechanism. Where a
narrative fact has a build consequence, the ticket is linked and the consequence is
left there.

## 1. The premise, and what is actually being tested

**The Archons built this universe as a simulation, and they built it as a test.** [SETTLED]
They are not gods in the sense of having created everything; they are a higher order
that authors lower ones. They do not want worship, tribute or obedience. **They are
farming for potential** — they build dimensions, seed them, and watch whether
anything inside can climb out under its own power. They think it is fun.
`COSMOLOGY.md` §1, `WAYSTONE.md` §1.

**The tower is the eras.** [SETTLED] Neolithic, Medieval, Industrial, Spacer, Ultra,
Archotech, and then out. Six floors and a door. Going through every era in order is
not a pacing convention — it is the shape of the test. *A thing that arrives at the
top carried is not a thing that climbed.* This is why the Archons seeded a world and
left rather than granting power to whoever they liked: granted power measures
nothing.

**The pass condition is not strength.** [SETTLED] Strength is common — the
Glitterites hold galaxies of it and are going nowhere. What is measured is whether a
thing can take on that much power without losing hold of what the power was ever
for. *The mark is not a gift, it is an invitation to be measured*, and every Archon
intervention since has been a probe to see whether the power has eaten them yet.

**Transcendence is graduation, not escape.** [SETTLED] You become more than this
reality can hold, you see its rules for the illusions they always were, and you step
through. *The burdens do not end because you outlasted them. They end because you
transcended them.*

**The Archons intervene only when the experiment stalls.** [SETTLED] Never to help
and never to rescue. Every such intervention is one of the campaign's beats. The
first and largest was the marking.

**Deep time is resolved, and it is a gift rather than a problem.** [SETTLED]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §2. The world was
seeded long ago and then **stalled at Neolithic for a very long time; the mark is the
shove.** So there is as much deep history as any fiction needs — ancient trees, an
ancient church, millennia of drift — and then the whole tower is climbed at
impossible speed once the shove lands. Conrad: *"Time is not linear or perceived to
be… 'one year' just kind of means 'a long freaking time'. Don't nitpick that."*

**And the compression is evidence, not an artifact.** [SETTLED] Nobody should live
through the Neolithic and the Industrial in one lifetime. The founders do. The
impossible pace of history is the loudest anomaly in the game, already there, free,
for the noticing.

## 2. The founders

**Adam and Eve with the fall already behind them.** [SETTLED] Two people marked by
god, dropped into a broken world, given the smallest taste of divine power and shown
a path to more. The archetype is deliberate.

**They were marked by hand, undiluted, at the source.** [SETTLED] Not a stronger
dose of what everyone has — a **different provenance**. Their line was seeded
directly by something from above the world, in person, and the rest of the world's
biology went on without it. `COSMOLOGY.md` §5.

**They are the protagonists and they are the progress bar.** [SETTLED] How far into
the story you are can be read off their gene list.

**They are hard to kill, not hard to hurt.** [SETTLED] Losing your main pawns to one
bad afternoon is not fun and the design refuses it outright. But they lose limbs and
eyes and months of usefulness. Overpowered pieces have to be spent carefully in a
world that can genuinely maim them.

**They stay singular through the mark, not through deathlessness.** [SETTLED] Every
vanilla sanguophage is deathless; that cannot be the distinction. What the founders
carry is a **mark**: the Archons touched them and nobody else.

> *I can make you like me. I cannot make you one of Theirs.*

**They must drink because their biology runs on a concentration this world does not
contain.** [SETTLED] The most important emotional fact in the campaign, and it is
never a curse and never a disease.

> *They are not sick. They are correctly built for somewhere else.*

Bread feeds the part of them that belongs here. Nothing that grows on this planet
feeds the rest. Blood is the nearest available substitute, which is exactly why it
works, why it is never sufficient, and why the hunger always returns.
`COSMOLOGY.md` §5.

**Their capacity outran their supply the day they were marked.** [SETTLED] They were
opened wider at the dawn of the world than anything on this planet can fill.
`COSMOLOGY.md` §7.

**⚠ Every gene from the chain makes them stronger and hungrier in the same motion.**
`COSMOLOGY.md` §5 states the progression curve and the horror are the same line on
the same graph. The archive states the opposite. See §12.2.

**Two founders, symmetric, and everything doubles.** [SETTLED]
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §7. The campaign is
authored as a single narrative and then doubled for co-op — double the quest, or two
of the thing at the site. *"We're not engineering the campaign itself to expect
multiplayer. We're building the campaign in the narrative, and then simply doubling
it, so two people are walking inside by side."* This closes `QUESTLINE.md` §8's
"why two rather than one".

**Both walk through the door.** [SETTLED] Transcendence is not a single seat.
*"The whole point of this is to get your Adam and Eve to transcend, and both players
should be able to do that."*

**Divergence lives on the augment vectors and the psychic tree**, [SETTLED] where it
is each player's own choice and own practice rather than a negotiation at the table.
Core vectors both founders get.

**Never write what the founders believe.** [SETTLED] No canonical creed, no
scripture, no conviction placed in a protagonist's mouth. Those two pawns are the
player's to roleplay, and a hardcoded inner life takes the seat they were meant to
sit in. `cosmology-in-practice.md` §2.

**⚠ How many were marked is deliberately open.** [OPEN] `COSMOLOGY.md` §1 says *"a
handful of hominids"*; `WAYSTONE.md` §2 says *"these two and nobody else"*. Conrad,
in [#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §8: *"the case is
open. How many were touched? Who? Where? Nobody knows. Archons are fickle bitches
and they do what they want — often in secret."* Deliberate, not a defect. It leaves
the door open for another marked line without committing to one. See §12.3.

## 3. anima, the altar, and the price

**The Archons did not put their essence into people. They put it into the world.**
[SETTLED] Before there was history, their blood was merged into the biological
substrate of this universe. Everything descended from that substrate carries a
trace. Grass carries it. Muffalo carry it. Every raider on every map carries it, and
it is vanishingly thin in all of them.

Call the trace **anima**. It is not a fuel and it is not a magic. It is the life of
the universe itself, in the only quantity this world was given.

**Everything not descended from that seeding has none**, [SETTLED] and no amount of
sophistication puts it there. A windmill has no anima. Neither does a reactor, a
gravship, or a mechanoid. *They are not descended from anything.*

**So the whole practice is reconcentration.** [SETTLED] Nobody here creates
divinity. They re-collect what was deliberately scattered, out of the only things
that ever held it. Blood is where anima sits, and blood is the only form in which it
leaves a body intact.

Everything the altar does follows from that one fact — `COSMOLOGY.md` §3:

| The rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| It takes a whole life | A person is almost entirely world and barely anima. You take all of one to get a trace of the other. |
| The charge never spoils | It is what life is made of, not a current in a wire. It does not decay or disperse. |
| A windmill cannot make it | Nothing that was not born carries any. |
| Power makes blood go further | Better apparatus loses less. Electricity is overhead, never substance. |
| The price escalates forever | Purity is exponential. Every rung needs disproportionately more source material than the last. |

**Animals give a taste and never more.** [SETTLED] Enough to take the edge off a
founder's hunger in a bad winter; never enough to make anything.

**Technology is a closed system, and that makes it apparatus.** [SETTLED] Everything
anyone can build is made of parts sourced from inside this universe, and this
universe is precisely the thing being escaped. Anima is the sole imported ingredient
in the experiment and the only reason anything ever gets out. Technology cannot add
anything; it only refines what you already have — *the difference between a hole in
the ground and an instrument you engineer your own divinity with*, which is four of
the six eras.

**One machine, one philosophy.** [SETTLED] There is exactly one *path* to becoming
an Archon and it runs on blood: the machine, the sacrifices, and the discipline that
grows out of them. Read that as one philosophy, not one mechanism — the genes and the
psychic track are both on it, because both are paid for the same way and neither can
be had anywhere else. What is refused is a **rival device**: a second way of buying
power that does not cost a life. When a mechanic needs extending, extend the altar.
Amended into `WAYSTONE.md` §3 by
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10).

**Power arrives late and never replaces blood.** [SETTLED] It makes blood go
further, and that is worse rather than better: cheaper lives mean more of them. The
machine ends the campaign holding two charges at once, and only one of them is the
price.

**The price is always paid by somebody else.** [SETTLED] The person in the vat dies.
The person receiving the gene never does. That asymmetry is the horror of it, and it
is also what makes the machine safe to gamble with.

**The altar starts as religion and ends as industry.** [SETTLED] `WAYSTONE.md` §3,
ratified unchanged by `QUESTLINE.md` §1 and by
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §3.

| Era | What the altar is to your people |
| --- | --- |
| Neolithic | An object that demands blood. You understand nothing. It refuses you and you never learn why. |
| Medieval | A rite. Not science — priesthood. You learn by repetition what pleases it. |
| Industrial | A machine. You work out what you have been doing for four hundred years, and do not stop. |
| Spacer / Ultra | An instrument. You are engineering your own divinity. |
| Archotech | A door. |

### The price, stated

**The single sentence this campaign is built on:** [SETTLED]

> **If you were good enough, you would not have to pay.**

The price is not punishment and it is not an appetite being fed. **It is the
exchange rate between what you are and what you are reaching for.** You spend other
people's lives because you are not yet enough to reach without them, and you know
that while you are doing it. That is what keeps the fantasy and the guilt in the same
hand.

**Efficiency never means mercy. It means scale.** [SETTLED] The rate improves and
the bill explodes. Risk falls across the campaign while cost rises far faster. By the
end the altar can barely fail, the body count is at its highest, and you are
acquiring people specifically to feed it.

**Determinism where it counts.** [SETTLED] Uncertainty about *how well* is welcome.
Uncertainty about *what you are getting* is not, and no dice roll may cost a good
pawn. Named rewards from the chain do not fail. Everything random is opt-in, on its
own path, priced as a gamble the player chose — including outcomes that are genuinely
bad, because a lottery with no bad band is not a lottery.

## 4. Taken and given

**Both are blood. What differs is the state the anima is in when it arrives.** [SETTLED]

> **Anima taken adds to what you have. Anima given adds to what you can hold.**

**Blood taken by force comes out tainted.** [SETTLED] A person dying unwillingly
floods everything in them with what they feel about it, and the anima carries that
out of the body along with itself. It does not stop being the life of the universe.
It stops being clean.

**Tainted anima still burns, and burns hot.** [SETTLED] It is entirely sufficient
for the blunt work: driving a gene into a body, feeding a founder, running a machine
built to take it. **Any life will do — willing or not, stranger or kin.** This is why
the sacrifice economy works exactly as designed and is never punished for working.

**Blood given willingly stays pure**, [SETTLED] and pure anima does the one thing
tainted anima never will.

### The channel

**Every living thing holds a little anima, and it calls back to its source.**
[SETTLED] Call that connection a **channel**. In almost everyone it is hair-thin and
they go their whole lives without knowing it is there.

**It is what limits anyone** — not how much anima you can be handed, but how much of
it can reach you. Pure anima opens the channel as it passes; tainted anima never
does, and no quantity changes that, because widening is not something force
accomplishes.

> **Nobody can be made larger. They can only be met halfway.**

| | What it changes | Whose blood serves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Archon genes** | What you are | Anyone. Tainted serves. |
| **The rite** | How much reaches you | A believer who means it, received by someone whose channel is clear. |

**The channel is the model under every psychic number.** [SETTLED] Psylink is how
wide it is. Psyfocus is how much is currently moving through. Neural heat is what
happens when more is drawn through than the channel can carry. Three stats, one
thing, seen from three angles — and it is the same sentence as `WAYSTONE.md` §2's
reflavour: *you are not overheating, you are processing more than human neurons can
carry.*

### The two hands

**Anima passes through two hands on its way into a person, and either one can waste
it.** [SETTLED] The giving hand can be built. The receiving hand cannot, and that is
where everything in this cosmology has ever failed.

**The giving is solvable and is meant to be solved.** [SETTLED] Purity is a question
about the person the blood came from and it has a straightforward answer: they have
to mean it. Nobody reaches that threshold without having been brought there by
somebody, over time, on purpose — **which is why the rite refuses a stranger without
needing a further rule.** Conversion and conviction are the sanctioned progression,
not a loophole: the min-maxed outcome here is a colony that built a real religion,
which is the intended one. `cosmology-in-practice.md` §3.

**The receiving is never a score.** [SETTLED] A clear channel is not a moral
achievement — not virtue, not gentleness, not restraint. It is a condition of a
person: unmistakable from the inside, impossible to check from the outside, and
genuinely hard to name, in the way *enlightenment* and *nirvana* and *love* are hard
to name.

> *A channel stays clear in someone who is still in contact with what is on the other
> side of it.*

**Nothing about it is tracked, scored, penalised or enforced.** [SETTLED] A hidden
purity meter is the one implementation guaranteed to ruin this — anything tracked
becomes a number, anything numbered becomes optimised, and a channel that can be
optimised was never a channel. `cosmology-in-practice.md` §4.

**A channel clouds by drift, and it can drift in any direction.** [SETTLED] Not by
killing — this colony kills constantly, at scale, on purpose, and is right to.
Cruelty is only the obvious direction. **Zeal narrows a channel as surely as
indifference does**, and so do despair, appetite, terror, grief held past its season,
and certainty. At least one of the failures depicted in the world should be
sympathetic.

**The drift that matters for this campaign is habituation.** [SETTLED] The moment a
person stops registering as one, and the moment a belief stops being believed and
becomes a thing you do on Tuesdays.

> *You are not being asked to kill less. You are being asked to never stop knowing the
> weight of your actions, as you continue to take them.*

**Somewhere around the hundredth time, when nobody is reading the names on the intake
list any more, the campaign's real antagonist arrives and it is the player.**
[SETTLED] — and it is delivered by pacing and prose and by nothing else. `cosmology-in-practice.md`
§8 names this the document's thinnest place: **habituation has no carrier.** If the
late-campaign beats do not land it, nothing else in the project will.

**Feeding is not wielding.** [SETTLED] Founders drink taken blood constantly and it
clouds nothing. Tainted anima keeps you alive and makes you strong; only the pure
grade makes you larger. The hunger stays morally neutral.

**And the test recurses.** [SETTLED] The balance that separates the two antagonists
at the scale of a civilisation is the same test running inside a single person, and
it applies to everyone who takes the rite, disciples included.

### Worship is bloodletting

**Worship in this world is not prayer. It is blood, physically given, by everyone, in
every faction that has worked it out.** [SETTLED] A believer opens a vein for their
god on a schedule and goes back to work. Reverence without blood does nothing; blood
without reverence is just blood.

**The two engines are the same substance in different economies:** [SETTLED]

- **Sacrifice** is a lump sum. Everything a person has, once, and they are gone.
- **Worship** is an annuity. A little, on a schedule, forever, and they live.

A colony runs both. Neither replaces the other.

**A body bleeds for one altar.** [SETTLED] Allegiance is not sentiment, it is
throughput, and every convert gained by one side is subtracted from the other.
**Conversion is zero-sum literally, not thematically**, and both sides can do the
arithmetic from the first meeting.

**Believers are visible.** [SETTLED] Scarred forearms. You can read someone's
allegiance off their arms across a negotiation table, and the marking is legible from
the Neolithic to orbit without changing.

## 5. The ascent track — four teachers, in order

**Each arrives before the player knows what to do with it.** [SETTLED]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §3.

| | What it teaches | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The anima tree** | that anima exists at all — you find one, or you find people who worship one, and studying it teaches the basics | Neolithic, found |
| **The altar** | nothing, at first. It demands blood, refuses you, and you never learn why | Neolithic, from the Chronicle |
| **The first vector** | what it was for. A person goes in. **This is the moment the campaign explains itself** | Neolithic, from the Chronicle |
| **Worship** | that there is another kind of power, and it must be **given** | Medieval, from the Church |

**The gap between the altar arriving and the vector arriving is load-bearing, not
atmosphere.** [SETTLED] It *is* the Waystone's Neolithic line. The tree fills it: you
hold one thing that works and one that does not, and the difference between them is
the campaign's first real question.

**The slow path is real and it caps at one.** [SETTLED] `COSMOLOGY.md` §4: an anima
tree is a natural accumulator and the old traditions were right about all of it —
they found the one place in the world where anima pools on its own and built a
religion around not disturbing it. A tribe that finds a tree, keeps it quiet and waits
will genuinely get somewhere. It will take three hundred years and it will never
scale.

> *There was always a way that cost nobody anything. It just would not have gotten you
> out.*

**The slow path was sufficient for shamans. It is not sufficient for gods.**
[SETTLED] You did not choose blood because it was the only option. You chose it
because it was faster, and the door is very far up, and you are in a hurry. The
altar's arrival is therefore a **choice**, not the only road.

**⚠ The tree is gated to the old people; the altar is gated to nobody.** [SETTLED as
intent, contradicted as mechanism] The slow path belongs to those born to it; the
fast one is open to anyone willing to pay. The *intent* is settled; the meditation-focus
mechanism the two tickets picked to express it is contradicted. See §12.1.

**The other routes to psychic power are cut.** [SETTLED]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §6. The psylink
neuroformer is out — the cosmology supplies the reason, since nothing manufactured
was ever descended from anything. The blinding ritual is out: coherent, but a third
method nobody needs. A royal title is out as a route; the ladder is kept, the
Empire's grantorship is not. **The altar is the sole writer of psylink rank.**

## 6. Who rises, and how far

**Ordinary colonists rise as far as their commitment carries them.** [SETTLED] A
disciple who has genuinely mastered a discipline is a character, not a footnote. What
separates the founders is not a **ceiling** on everyone else but a difference in
**kind**: there are gifts only the marked may receive, and no amount of climbing
reaches them.

> Two chosen ones who can mint copies of themselves are not chosen — but two chosen
> ones surrounded by useless people are not interesting either.

**Disciples are real and they are reconstituted.** [SETTLED] A colonist lifted by the
altar receives anima that was collected, refined and put into them: genuinely
powerful, genuinely a character, and a copy of a copy. The founders' difference is
**provenance**, not a ceiling.

**Gate the ammunition, never the pawn.** [SETTLED]
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10). A gate on the
*recipient* tells the player *you may use this, but only there* — and they immediately
use it there, which is usually the worst place. A gate on the *item* means the
temptation never forms.

Three ammunition classes:

| Ammunition | Source | Who it works on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core vector** | Core questline only. Hand-authored, guaranteed, doubled so both founders get every one. | **Founders only** — the one hard gate. |
| **Augment vector** | Tier-2 and side quests. Randomised *which*, never *whether*. | **Anyone.** |
| **Lottery capsule** | A hoarded Archon capsule converted by Industrial research. | **Anyone.** |

**Augment vectors are open to anyone, deliberately.** [SETTLED] Two founders cannot
specialise in everything and do not have the hours. *It takes a village, literally.*
Narratively: an Archon vector is a determined thing, made by gods, guaranteed to work
— unlike the lottery, which is made by man and therefore imperfect.

**The mark has two jobs.** [SETTLED] It gates the core vectors and Transcendence, and
it carries a small permanent presence — flavour, social or presence-shaped, never a
stat to optimise. It is explicitly **not** a ceiling on every track. *Which* vectors
and paths it gates is deferred to
[the power grid](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/31).

**Rejected and not to be revived:** [SETTLED] the founders implanting their own
gating genes into a colonist to mint a new demigod. *"I don't overly like that
narrative."* Also dead: reimplantation of the founders' xenotype onto converts
(`QUESTLINE.md` §3), for the same reason.

**Discipleship is psychic only.** [SETTLED] It changes nothing about a pawn's
genetics. *The powers come from God because you worship God.* Entry and re-entry are
gated on **belief, not attendance** — a slave converted at full certainty is a zealot
and qualifies immediately; belief is the qualification, not seniority. Devotion is
required to **advance**, never to **retain**: a disciple whose faith lapses keeps what
they have and stops climbing until they are right with the church again.

**Disciples have no rank ceiling — only a narrower set of paths.** [SETTLED] They can
max anything open to them. The founders stay above them by holding **paths nobody
else may enter**, plus the core gene set. *"Disciples rise but never arrive" is
structural, not a rule, and there is no cap to police.*

**⚠ Which era discipleship opens in is contradicted.** See §12.5.

**The path is followers, not service.** [SETTLED]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §4. Conrad: *"The path
is not titles and honor. It's worship. It's all about the number of followers you
have, and each of them is making blood sacrifices to you regularly, and some of them
willingly give themselves, goes to you to fuel you."* **Titles become tiers of
godhood, and what pays for a tier is willing human sacrifice.** Nothing else.

**We grant the tiers ourselves.** [SETTLED] No shuttle, no bestower, no foreign lord.
Conrad: *"I don't want to feel like I'm subservient to somebody bestowing these things
on me."* His picture of the moment: *"I step out in front of the crowd, this guy parts
and for me, and light shines down from the heavens, and you know I hover above the
ground and become more divine."*

**A follower-count gate does not exist as a question.** [SETTLED] *"This is a
roleplay issue, not a logistics issue. You're not literally going to have thousands
of pawns that follow."* **The volunteer count is the congregation measurement** — the
reason you can get five extremely devout pawns to walk into the altar is that your
religion is that potent.

**⚠ The victim of the rite: willing follower, or converted prisoner?** See §12.6.

**Parked, and agreed as better:** [OPEN] **use-based advancement** — practice a power
to advance in it. *"If I want to advance in my ability to use pyrokinetic psychic
powers, I should have to go practice with those powers."* Deferred as too deep for
now; revisit after playtesting.

## 7. The world, and the clock

**The planet's tech level starts low and advances with you. Every faction *of this
world* is on that clock. Spacefaring civilisations not of this world are exempt.**
[SETTLED] [#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §1.

Conrad: *"You can imagine this as the world is neolithic, but the universe is not…
And that arc would be broken if your next-door neighbor is driving around in tanks,
because then you just feel like a retarded tribal civilization and the rest of the
world has already existed and outpaced you. And so what's the point?"*

> **There is nothing above you on this planet, because nobody on this planet has been
> anywhere you have not.**

**The starting state is staggered, not uniform.** [SETTLED]
[#8](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/8) §1 — Conrad's decision,
against a recommendation that everyone start Neolithic:

- **The bulk of the map is Neolithic tribes at low settlement weight** — nomadic, small, unremarkable.
- **A few powers are already early-Medieval.** They have set down cities and learned to farm. They are ahead of you and they **do not care about you**, because you are beneath their notice. *"It's Rome and Carthage."*
- **Nothing planetside is Industrial or above.**

*"You have China, Persia, Rome — these are some of the ancient powers throughout
history. While they were amassing an empire, other people were toiling in the dirt.
But by the time you reached the Medieval period a lot of people were much closer. The
gap had narrowed."* And the alternative was refused in his own words: *"I don't want
the Africa spawn RNG."*

**How many powers, and how much map each holds, is
[The faction grid](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/34)'s** —
"two or three" and "10–15%" are inferences from a single worked example, not figures
he gave.

**Any civilisation above your tier must be in orbit — but a high-tech power may hold
strongholds on the surface.** [SETTLED] A stronghold is a **place, not a neighbour**:
no diplomacy, no trade, no goodwill, out of the incident pool until you are in band,
and murderous if approached. Conrad: *"they're weird little alien gods, and nobody
fucks with them… Scenery with teeth, just so for those who are inquisitive enough to
go looking."* It gives the Neolithic map a locked door you can walk to on day 40 and
learn nothing from.

**Exempt factions are exempt from the clock, never from the band.** [SETTLED] They
exist at full tech from day one and reach you only through **authored** contact — a
beat, a quest, a trade visit we wrote. The intended feeling: their settlement is on
your world map from day one, you can caravan to it in the Neolithic, it is real and
enormous, **and it will not open the door.**

**The world only ever moves at an era transition.** [SETTLED]
[#8](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/8) §2. Not a daily tick —
*"you wave the magic wand."* The era rite fires and the world visibly ages in one
moment: some Neolithic factions climb, the powers swallow settlements, the map
redraws. **No new location is ever invented**; worldgen places every settlement that
will ever exist, and what changes is who owns it and what tier they are.

**Only the Neolithic→Medieval movement is authored up front.** [SETTLED] After it,
*"it depends on the quest line, the character, or the player is going through, and who
they decide to ally with and not, and we expand from there."* The later columns of the
faction grid are **branches, not a timeline**.

**The narrative this buys, in his words:** *"suddenly the Catholic Church faction has
taken over thirty-five percent of the map, and the narrative there is this has been
happening for hundreds of years that you have been advancing to the medieval era as
well, and they didn't care about you, and they didn't talk to you because they were
bigger than you."*

**Why a faction fades: stalled is the default, absorbed is an authored event.**
[SETTLED] *"Some civilizations are simply retarded and they couldn't figure out the
next technology, and so they stayed put and became irrelevant. Other civilizations
were conquered."* Neolithic factions that do not climb **shrink** — the superpowers
gobble the map — so by the middle-to-end of the Medieval only two or three tiny
Neolithic tribes remain. **A Neolithic tribe still sitting in the same valley in your
Industrial era is the campaign's own thesis, visible on the world map, and still
raidable for the altar.** Absorbed is told in one letter at a chapter close, never
simulated.

**You only pay for the climb of a faction the player has already met.** [SETTLED]
Everyone else climbed offscreen, for free, and you meet them mid-climb. *"You probably
discover new ones that you didn't know before, but the narrative is they've been on
the same path as you."*

**Diplomacy in the Neolithic is deliberately thin.** [SETTLED] The powers will not
talk to you until you are Medieval.

**The rest of the world is a political board, not a vending machine.** [SETTLED]
`WAYSTONE.md` §5. Factions should want things from each other and not only from you,
so that choosing one is refusing another. Standing moves on its own in response to
what you do and what you decline to do, and the movement is visible before it becomes
a crisis. Relationships form slowly and through encounter — you learn what a faction is
by meeting it, not by reading a menu. **Taking things by force is a first-class
verb.** And there is always a floor beneath total hostility — tribute, vassalage, a
humiliating peace — **because losing at politics should be a story, not a soft loss
screen.**

**Politics is the right pressure because it is the world showing your own reflection
back at you.** [SETTLED] [#5](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/5)
§4, and it is the load-bearing insight of that ticket:

> Some people see you as gods, some see monsters, and there are all the shades in
> between. It lands because you have your own moral and ideological struggle running at
> the same time — you have recruited pawns and slaves who do not share your religion,
> every sacrifice angers them, so you need stricter measures to keep them in line, and
> you convert most of what you recruit because otherwise they would never put up with
> you. **The external politics and the internal strain are the same story told twice.**

**The world is hard because the story says so.** [SETTLED] Two gods in a garden bring
the snake. Word gets around; raiders come, get destroyed, go home and tell everyone.
The intended feel is ordinary colonists dying to things the founders walk through, and
a constant churn of recruitment around two immortals. *You should lose people, not
runs.*

**No race bleeding.** [SETTLED] Archons and Starjacks must not seep into ordinary
planetside factions. A trace of the exotic is flavour; a world where everyone is
exotic is noise.

**Chapter closes.** [SETTLED] Political chapters end where the cast is replaced, and
two are structural: **the end of M3** closes the medieval political chapter, and **the
end of S1** closes the planetside one, at the larger scale, using the ship as the
instrument. **One or two factions climb with you across each close and the rest
fade** — which closes the Waystone's old open tension on whether allies can climb.

## 8. The cast

**The Archons — man behind the curtain.** [SETTLED] You barely see them. Hidden, no
diplomacy, they never raid, and the only thing they ever ask is *become one of us.*
Their otherness is **sacral, not void**: gods, not entities.
**⚠** The encounter budget is contested — see §12.4.

**The Glitterites — the villain, and the *take without give* pole.** [SETTLED] A
gatekeeper civilisation the Archons seeded and left behind, running on glitterworld
technology, with one instruction: *let nothing out of this system that could not have
taken the system by force.* Glitterworld tech first, Archon tech after. Mostly in
orbit; planetside only as scars. **Permanently hostile on purpose — a gate you can
negotiate with is not a gate.**

Their fall came first and everyone applauded it. [SETTLED] `COSMOLOGY.md` §10: a
civilisation of the profoundly convicted — a body is a substrate, a mind is a process,
sentiment is noise in the measurement. They removed sentiment from their
decision-making deliberately, across centuries, every step correct and defensible when
it was taken. **Becoming androids was the last of those steps, not a separate fact.**
What went out with the noise was the channel — not damaged, not clouded, but **closed,
at the root, by their own best minds.**

They understood anima better than anyone ever has and farmed it in every way it could
be farmed: whole galaxies converted to carefully invented ideologies, facilities the
size of planets, millions of vats growing clones whose only purpose was to be a
battery. **And it was pure.** They hoarded more anima than all other civilisations
combined, at a purity nobody has reached before or since, and it didn't matter. So
they run a loop that cannot terminate:

> *Obtain anima. Burn it. Observe no ascent. Conclude the shortfall was quantity.
> Obtain more anima.*

**Their envy is not of your power** — they hold more than the founders ever will. It is
envy of your *potential*, visible to them, working, in people wearing leather, and
amputated from themselves centuries ago.

> **They did not hit a wall. They became one.**

**The mechs have become the means.** [SETTLED] Always on task, executing an
instruction with no termination condition. **Mechanites are the storage half** — a
lattice grown inside a body to hold what flesh cannot, which works exactly as designed.
A lattice has no channel and never will, so it can never give anything to anything. *A
colonist coming down with fibrous mechanites in year two has been touched by a machine
that is still, patiently, doing its job.*

**They wield Archon artifacts as machines** — **gear without gnosis**, a frightening
thing to fight at the top of the tower and an exact portrait of the failure.

**The Church — the rival climber, and the *give without take* pole.** [SETTLED]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §5. Conrad: *"Empire →
Church (think catholic church during the medieval period). The reason the church is
able to become divine at all is through the willing sacrifice of followers."* Huge
following, vast willing bloodletting, probably ruled by vampires, deeply corrupt.

**It is planetside and on the world clock** — a fellow traveller that climbs from the
Neolithic alongside you, not an offworld visitor.

Its congregation is sincere: millions of genuine gifts, freely given, exactly right,
generation after generation. **They solved the giving half completely.** And the men at
the top farm them — *they still recite a creed that never named an Archon and worked
for centuries anyway, and they stopped believing it somewhere back down the line.*
Nothing was removed; it simply stopped being meant, and what has stopped being meant
does not reach anything. **They can elevate. They cannot make gods. They do not know
that is what they are missing.**

**Which hands you the conversion story: the believers are doing it correctly.**
[SETTLED] They are giving something real into a channel that closed generations ago,
and you can offer them somewhere it actually lands.

**The relationship is mutual use, not service.** [SETTLED] Explicitly refused: the
*serve the Empire, then defect* beat the source mods ship. Conrad: *"I don't like the
idea of our Founders serving anyone else, but definitely maybe being misled or abused
by somebody else… the Catholic Church tried to hold you up or use you as a symbol to
strengthen themselves by proxy, and then holds the cards to also stab you in the back
whenever it serves them."* Not a concordat of equals and not service — **both parties
counting.**

**The finale follows:** the Church climbs with you, the Industrial era is where you
outgrow being useful to them, and the Spacer close is the last planetside war before
orbit.

**The Deserters — the other climber, and the one you may ally with instead.**
[SETTLED that they exist and are the alternative; DRAFT on who they are]
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21): *"I know the least
about them, and this is the story that we have fleshed out the least."*

- **They start inside the Church and split at the Medieval crossing.** [SETTLED] *"Probably it is part of the rome/carthage faction, then narratively it 'splits off' when we reach the medieval age. They believe the church is corrupt, naturally in tension with each other."* Carthage is not a peer at the founding; it becomes one, and you watch it happen.
- **The candidate identity, offered and not decided:** [DRAFT] **the Deserters are the congregation that noticed** — who left because they could tell nothing was landing. That would make the forced choice hard rather than good-versus-evil: millions of believers with a hollow socket at the top, against a few thousand who mean every drop. Owned by [The Church and the Deserters](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/32).

**The player is forced to choose between the Church and the Deserters.** [SETTLED]
They are the ally/reject pair.

**The trade ally — the third climber, and never a forced choice.** [SETTLED]
[#8](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/8) §7. A **distinct third
faction**, not either of the two that already have a posture toward you. Conrad,
correcting a proposal to fold it into another faction: *"this is not the first witness.
This is a tiny, friendly little faction that trades a few useful items, and if you so
choose to you can invest in them. NOT the faction that loves you OR the faction that
hates you."*

- **Its character is a constraint, not flavour:** *"somebody that you're not relying on for any military help, that isn't going to serve you or do favors for you like that, but that you can always trade with and you can build goodwill and get better trade prices over the course of the game."* **No military dimension, owes you no service.**
- **Met mid-Neolithic, once caravans start.** It is the only faction you form trade-and-alliance ties with in that era.
- **Its story closes at the planet-to-space boundary: you sponsor it into the Traders Guild.** *"The merchant faction survives because we protect it (or it dies because we don't!) and if we take the time, we help them get to orbit and join the traders guild in the sky."*

**The Starjack Free Companies — the ally.** [SETTLED] Independent crews who hold that
a ship belongs to the people aboard it and nobody planetside gets a say. A spacefaring
faction with real teeth, which you can ally with *and* go to war with.

**The anima tribe.** [SETTLED as intent] A Neolithic faction that worships in anima,
met through a questline, able to raid. It is **authored into the roster**; the
questline delivers the meeting, not the faction.

**Two Neolithic postures exist from day one: one faction adores you, one hates you.**
[DRAFT] Working names **the First Witness** and **the Ash-Marked**, carried from
[#8](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/8)'s earlier sessions and
referenced in its resolution. Their *contents* — names, character, everything — belong
to [The faction grid](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/34). One
constraint on them is settled: *"by having the First Witness be something that climbed
from the Neolithic era, you're getting me attached to somebody I've probably
obliterated"* — **so the Neolithic factions stay Neolithic.**

**The two poles, and why neither can see it.** [SETTLED] Everything that has ever
failed this test failed by leaning: take without give, or give without take. Both
antagonists are dominant on their own axis and permanently stalled on the other. **The
founders are the only thing in this universe running both at once, and that balance is
the exam.** Neither can perceive it, for the same reason in both cases: *each is
reaching for something it has made itself unable to reach.* The Glitterites see
primitives with a trick. The Church sees heretics with a machine. **Each is staring
directly at their own missing half.**

**[OPEN] The shape of the tension between the two climbers is unwritten**, and
`COSMOLOGY.md` §10 holds it open deliberately: *do not invent it in a quest.* Owned by
[The Church and the Deserters](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/32).

## 9. The arc, era by era

**Fourteen leaps and a coda.** [SETTLED]
[#5](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/5). Narrative shape only —
no research lists, no items, no day counts.

| | Leap | What the player is feeling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **N1** | **The Awakening** | Cavemen in a circle working out what a rock is. Every discovery hands over a *verb*. Deliberately fast. Ends at the **First Thought**. |
| **N2** | **The Primitive Baseline** | Now build the worst version of everything, and every pawn is bad at all of it. That is the point. |
| **N3** | **The Settled Tribe** | Rome without the metal: walled, disciplined, fed, armoured in hardened leather. Then you learn that wood burns. |
| **M1** | **The Forge** | Metal. Everything you built for a hundred days gets remade in it. The largest single leap in the project. |
| **M2** | **Mail & Siege** | You stop being new at metal. Raids stop being frightening and start being *work*. |
| **M3** | **Plate & Powder** | The best the era offers, and an army to put it on. The meat of this leap is what the world demands, not what you unlock. |
| **I1** | *Late 1800s* | Electricity overhauls everything at once. Too few capable pawns; prioritise ruthlessly. **Scrambling to keep up.** |
| **I2** | *1950s* | Deep drilling and scanners end your resource anxiety. **Feeling good about yourself.** |
| **I3** | *Modern → dystopian* | Power armour, bionics, charged shot, tanks, aircraft. **Good, but you don't quite have everything.** |
| **I4** | **The Capstone** | The best industrial arms and armour — **and the gravship.** An okay ship by the end. |
| **S1** | **The Crown** | You use the ship to finish the planet. Vassals, territory, scores settled, the founders as kings. |
| **S2** | **The Departure** | The colony lives aboard. Diplomacy moves off-world. |
| **U1** | **The Probe** | You meet something you cannot read. A fight you barely survive, and all you take home is a piece of it. |
| **U2** | **The Arsenal** | Reverse-engineering into parity, then past it. |
| **—** | **The coda** | Not an era. Nothing changes: all the old dangers are still there and you are equipped for every one of them. |

**Archotech is not a research era.** [SETTLED] **Ultra is the ceiling of the tree.**
Archotech is what you *find*, and it belongs to the Archons — putting archotech in
anyone else's hands quietly refutes the premise that the only archotech in this
universe belongs to the beings who wrote the universe.

**Named moments are dropped as a design unit.** [SETTLED] Beats and moments fall out
of the story; they are not a quota. **First Thought, First Wall, First Ingot, First
Spark** stand because they emerged that way.

**Industrial is the longest era**, [SETTLED] plausibly four in-game years on its own.
**Days follow leaps: an era is long because it has more to hand over, never because we
are holding you there.** ⚠ On total length, see §12.8.

### What the founders gain

[SETTLED] — as a **feeling**, not a gene count.

| Era | The feeling |
| --- | --- |
| **Neolithic** | **Gods who can bleed.** One founder mows through five men in melee. But explosives kill them, fire takes limbs, and a lost limb is gone. |
| **Medieval** | **The world bends around me.** Factions arrive with worship and tribute; others declare you sacrilegious demons. **The heaviest gains.** |
| **Industrial** | **I am smart. I am scientific.** Their thinnest era for raw power — the real unlock is other people. |
| **Spacer** | **Space cannot conquer me.** The vacuum means nothing to someone who cannot die and needs no oxygen, food or water. |
| **Ultra** | **Unstoppable — against enemies who are almost unfairly strong.** The enemy's strength feels unbalanced. That is the point: the Archons put them there as the obstacle. |
| **Coda** | The power fantasy, lived in. |

### What the world does back

[SETTLED]

| Era | What the world is |
| --- | --- |
| **Neolithic** | **Curious and cruel.** Worshipped by some, abominated by others, and neither protects you. |
| **Medieval** | **Ideological and political.** Armies that arrive addressed to the founders by name, with things that break walls. |
| **Industrial** | **Ahead of you.** The anxiety of the real Industrial Revolution — they have had the printing press for fifteen years and you have ten percent literacy. |
| **Spacer** | **Finishable.** Every account opened since the Neolithic comes due, you write the ending, and then it goes quiet as you look up. |
| **Ultra** | **Unreadable.** Fear, then study, then parity, then it is unfair in your favour. |
| **Coda** | **The same as it was.** Still dangerous, and you are the answer to all of it. |

**Each era's pressure tends to invalidate the previous era's answer — a baseline, not
a law.** [SETTLED] The Neolithic's wooden walls do not survive the Medieval; nutrient
paste does not invalidate cooking.

**The run leaves residue.** [SETTLED] The throne room you built as a medieval king is
still yours in orbit — because you chose to keep it, not because the game denied you
something better.

**A domain ceilings when the need it serves stops finding better answers — not when
the need ends.** [SETTLED] Food is the worked example: largely finished by the end of
the Medieval, and that is correct, not a gap. **The ceiling table itself is
deliberately unwritten** — it is derived from the locked mod set, and the set is an
output of the map.

## 10. The Chronicle, as far as it is written

**A beat is always a place you travel to and take something from, never a parcel
dropped on your roof.** [SETTLED] `WAYSTONE.md` §6, `CONTEXT.md`. No drop-pod rewards
anywhere in the chain.

**The next mission is the hook.** [SETTLED] The founders' power arc is what holds six
hundred days together. *If the player ever stops thinking about the next beat, the
campaign has failed at that moment.*

**Rhythm: big site → several small and medium sites → big site.** [DRAFT] Marked
`[SETTLED]` in `QUESTLINE.md` §1 but never ratified outside the archive.

**Pacing is denser than the sixteen-beat draft** — more beats, most of them small.
[DRAFT] Same status. *A full year between beats means the player has forgotten the
altar exists.*

**Three quest tiers, and an Archon quest must never be visually confusable with an
ordinary one.** [SETTLED]
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §11. *"If it's an Archon
quest, it gets top priority immediately."*

1. **Core questline** — deterministic story beats at authored times, hand-placed loot.
2. **Tier-2 subquests** — **named rewards, known before you go**. *You are going to get the perfect shooting vector.*
3. **Random side quests** — shorter, storyteller-rolled, loot unknown.

**The dice roll on the reward, never on the pawn.** [SETTLED] The lottery is the one
exception and the player opted into it. A named reward is a hook; a mystery box is
not.

**Quests should feel alive** — not *"go here for free loot."* [DRAFT, archive]
Reward and danger belong on the same site.

### Act I — Neolithic

The era's content is **[SETTLED]** at
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §9: the founders get
**one Archon vector and one psylink level** across the whole era.

- **~end of N1:** a quest to sacrifice a willing follower of your religion, opening the psychic path. **The first sacrifice ritual is the Neolithic experience** — a ceremony you do not understand, performed on an object that demands blood, in front of a crowd.
- **~middle of N2:** the **genius gene**. Moved here from the end of the Medieval, on Conrad's call: *"giving it to them in the Neolithic solves the problem better, earlier."* Narratively correct too — **the Archons did not make them good at things, they made them *able to become* good at things**, which is what a fraction of divine DNA should do.

**Holding psychic power back to the Medieval was rejected.** [SETTLED] It would leave
the Neolithic altar doing exactly one thing and then sitting idle. The eras now stage
cleanly:

> **Neolithic you sacrifice and something happens to you; Medieval you build a
> cathedral around it and it happens to your people; Industrial you work out what it
> was doing the whole time and put it in a capsule.**

**The archive's three-beat Act I** [DRAFT] — marked `[SETTLED]` in `QUESTLINE.md` §1,
unratified, and **⚠ not reconciled with the schedule above** (see §12.9):

1. **The altar.** A large overground ancient complex, deserted or lightly guarded — one founder can clear it. Inside: the altar, and wall depictions of blood sacrifice. No instructions, no text, no explanation.
2. **A small site.** A teaser. Gives capsules and no answers. Skippable at no real cost.
3. **The first vector.** A small site. The moment the machine becomes real.

**The gap between beats 1 and 2 is doing real work and is not a beat.** [SETTLED as
intent, via [#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §3] The
altar accepts fuel from day one, so the player feeds it and nothing happens. They will
assume they are doing it wrong. **That silence is the Neolithic experience and it
costs nothing to build.**

### Act II — Medieval

**Small and medium sites through the middle.** [DRAFT, archive]

**The act closes on the Archon encounter, and the close is rewritten.** [SETTLED]
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §10 **supersedes**
`QUESTLINE.md` §1's version, in which the Archon phases out and you loot a vector from
the wreckage. What replaces it is a live conversation:

> **The Archon arrives and kills you.** You are deathless — that is the one thing in
> the world that can put a founder down, and it is the only way to *show* the gap
> between demigod and god rather than assert it. Both founders take it under the
> doubling rule. **You get up, because you are deathless and he knew that.**

**What he leaves you is the ability to charge a battery by touch.** [SETTLED] You walk
home and use it on the altar, the only thing you own that could take power. **That is
the first electricity in the campaign, and it happens before you have researched any.**
Charging the altar unlocks a class of vectors that cost power *and* blood, and you
receive the first one here — power does not replace blood, it makes blood go further.
Then you understand electricity well enough to study it, and the Industrial opens.

**Why the genetics half is not here.** [SETTLED] The Industrial is where **you** work
out what you have been doing for four hundred years. If the Archon simply tells you,
you worked nothing out. **He gives the *method* — the idea that the world runs on
something measurable — not the answers.** *More Enki, not less: the* me *are the
decrees of civilisation, not a list of facts.*

### Act III onward — the Industrial hinge

**The capsule hoard converts in the Industrial.** [SETTLED]
[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §2. Archon capsules drop
from the Neolithic onward and are **inert** — no gate says you may not use them, there
is simply nothing to put one in. Then: ordinary gene research → an **Archon quest** →
research that converts a hoarded capsule into a **lottery capsule**.

> The knowledge still comes from the gods, but you had to build the whole industrial
> base and finish a research line before you were permitted to ask the question.

**The lottery: you may not decline.** [SETTLED] Put a pawn in, pull the lever, take
one of the four. That is the downside, and it is what the player opted into. This
**reverses** `QUESTLINE.md` §5's suggested compromise. The four options must be
**coherent with the roll**: *"if I roll a shit lottery I'm okay getting shit genes…
[what would be] unfair [is] roll a good lottery and get a bunch of shitty options."*
Capsules are **categorised** rather than one pool — you know the *domain* of the gamble
but not the outcome. Bad genes are bad on their own merits: *"my pawn just rolled Tender
Skin and now if he touches fire he's gonna die."*

**Acts III, IV, V and VI are unwritten.** [OPEN] `QUESTLINE.md` marks them
`[MODEL]` and did not draft them.
[Act III — the Industrial act](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/38)
is ticketed; IV–VI are fog on the map, graduating one at a time.

**Archon encounter budget.** ⚠ See §12.4.

## 11. The register, and how the fiction reaches the player

**The register is sacral and gothic with an edge of realism.** [SETTLED]
[#6](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/6). Conrad:

> *"Cosmic horror doesn't fit. We're moving through the eras of human history into a
> dystopian future of glitterite technology and beyond, playing as vampire gods who
> eventually transcend the universe. We're looking more gothic. Think Roman Catholic
> Church meets Vlad the Impaler, not cosmic core."*

**The medieval-creature mods are in for *demonic* flavour, not eldritch.** [SETTLED]
Cosmic horror, void material and reality-warping strangeness are refused outright, and
the refusal is on **design**, not on price — it does not reopen. **The Archons'
otherness is sacral, not void: gods, not entities.** The vampire reading is to be
leaned into rather than softened.

**Vampires are the same phenomenon, further down the line.** [SETTLED] Somewhere far
back an Archon touched one, and it has been passed hand to hand ever since, thinning at
every step. They are old, they are real, and they are **a degraded inheritance rather
than a separate supernatural order.**

### The rules that govern every line of copy

**Nothing is ever stated plainly.** [SETTLED] `cosmology-in-practice.md` §1. No
letter, no quest description, no research blurb explains the tower, the experiment, or
the Archons' purpose. **The moment the fiction confirms its own premise, the best thing
about it dies.** The player is meant to assemble a version of the cosmology and be
permanently unsure whether they got it right. It arrives only as **anomaly** — things
that behave like a system rather than a world.

> **Write toward a grander scheme. Never name it.**

**The question of whether this reality is real stays open permanently.** [SETTLED]
*Two players arguing about it at three in the morning, both wrong in different ways, is
the target state.*

**Hand over the facts. Never hand over the meaning.** [SETTLED] The facts are given to
your faction and they narrow the field — the Archons marked this line by hand and have
come back since, so the colony knows what happened by **testimony rather than by
faith.**

**It is testimony, not consensus.** [SETTLED] Nobody else on the planet is obliged to
believe it, and mostly they do not. The church's account of how the world began has no
Archon in it. *A world where every religion is a different translation of one memo is a
world with one religion in it.*

**How it is told evolves; what is told does not.** [SETTLED] Thunder and gods in the
Neolithic. A liturgy in the Medieval. A translated text with three conflicting versions
in the Industrial. Someone in orbit reading the Neolithic version and realising it was
accurate all along. **The player watching the same truth survive four retellings is
doing more work than any exposition could.**

**Never adjudicate between faiths.** [SETTLED] The campaign knows which account is
factually correct and must never once say so, or reward it. **Being right about the
facts is not the qualification** — a believer who has never heard of an Archon can have
a channel wide open, and someone who has met one in person can be shut.

> **Knowing what happened is not the qualification. What you made of it is.**

**The ideology system is where the answer goes**, [SETTLED] and the campaign proceeds
as though it is the right one. Never validate it, never grade it, never imply a
different creed would have worked better.

> **The game does not check your answer. It checks that you answered.**

**Write the gap, never fill it.** [SETTLED] Refer to *what your people hold true*, to
conviction, to the thing the founders reach through — in the second person and in the
abstract, so it lands on whatever the player actually chose. **That phrasing is the
whole technique.**

**Written for a player who takes it seriously, and that assumption is allowed.**
[SETTLED] Designing around the player clicking through ideology setup at random would
mean scoring sincerity, and sincerity is exactly the thing that cannot be scored.

### Vocabulary

**One strange noun, permanently: `anima`.** [SETTLED] Everything else is plain English
— blood, the mark, the climb, the price, worship, sacrifice, taken, given, tainted,
pure, channel, clear, heart. *A world with one strange word is mystical; a world with
five is a rulebook.*

**Say tainted and say pure.** [SETTLED] And **avoid industrial metaphors for anima
entirely**: it is not ore, not material, not fuel. *Language that makes it sound like
refinery feedstock loses the only thing that makes the price cost anything.*

**Describe the channel, never specify it.** [SETTLED] A connection between a person and
the source, which has a width and a clarity. That is the whole of it. It never gets a
stated mechanism, a list of what obstructs it, a rating, or a diagram. **And do not
name the condition** — a name would make it a category, and a category invites a stat.

**Borrow the idea, never the words.** [SETTLED] No meridians, no core, no gates, no
dantian, no qi. ⚠ **`cultivation` is the exception and is canonical** — see §12.7.

**Sources for tone**, recorded so the register can be recovered: donghua
(interdimensional power struggle, cultivating power from a spiritual realm), manhwa
(the tower of trials; higher beings gift power, test it, and pull the cream into their
own machinations), **the Book of Enoch** (star people planting the genetic seeds of
humanity and teaching them to survive), and **The Matrix** (reality as a simulation,
where only by seeing beyond it can you bend rules that were never real).

## 12. Contradictions on the table

Recorded, not resolved. Each names both readings and where each came from.

### 12.1 The altar's meditation focus — two closed tickets, opposite verdicts, both marked verified

- **[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §4 (2026-09-01):** *"Author our own focus type; do not reuse vanilla's `Morbid`.* Morbid looks gothic out of the box and **is not usable**: seven traits claim it, which suppresses the 'open to all' fallback, so **only pawns with one of those traits can meditate to it at all.**"
- **[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §7 (2026-09-02):** *"the focus type `Morbid` carries **no requirement of any kind**. **[V]** No backstory, no title, nothing… So the altar's focus type is `Morbid`."*

The later ticket does not cite the earlier one and does not note that it is reversing
it. **The narrative line #21 hangs on it — *the tree is gated to the old people, the
altar is gated to nobody* — survives either way**, since #10's own-focus-type route
delivers the same split. What is contradicted is only which mechanism carries it.
Owned by whichever ticket next opens the psychic track.

### 12.2 Do power gains cost hunger?

- **`COSMOLOGY.md` §5:** *"Every gene from the chain makes them stronger and hungrier in the same motion. The more of the Archons they carry, the less this world can sustain them. **The progression curve and the horror are the same line on the same graph.**"*
- **`docs/archive/VISION.md`:** *"**Power gains carry no hunger cost.** Stacking archite genes drives metabolism down, which would quietly punish the exact progression the campaign is built on. **Cancelled deliberately.** The cost is measured in other people and nowhere else."*

VISION is archive and COSMOLOGY is live, so the *precedence* is clear — but VISION's
cancellation is implemented (`Archinity_ArchiteSustenance`), so the shipped behaviour
matches the archive and contradicts the live fiction.

**And a third input compounds it.**
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §4 flagged that the
founders' xenotype already ships `VRE_AnimalFeeder` and `VRE_Hemosmosis` — two hemogen
sources needing no pack and no prisoner. **The hunger may already be softer than the
design assumes.** #21 explicitly did not decide it and asked that it be priced wherever
the founders' xenotype is next opened.

### 12.3 How many were marked — deliberately open, and worth not closing by accident

`COSMOLOGY.md` §1 says *"a handful of hominids"* and §5 says *"their line"*;
`WAYSTONE.md` §2 says *"the Archons touched these two and nobody else."* Held open on
Conrad's explicit instruction. **A quest that answers it in passing has closed a door
he wanted open.**

### 12.4 The Archon encounter budget — two or three?

- **`WAYSTONE.md` §5:** *"You barely see them. **One or two encounters in a whole playthrough is plenty.**"*
- **[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §10:** *"**Archon encounter budget is now spent. Three**, all structurally different: the wordless opening that grants the mark, this conversation, and the door at the end. Waystone §5 says *you barely see them*. **No later act adds a fourth.**"*

#10 is later and explicit and cites the Waystone line, so it plausibly amends it — but
the Waystone was **not** edited to match, and #10's own list of Waystone edits does not
include §5. Note that the opening is the prehistoric marking, which the player never
witnesses in play, so *"two in play"* reconciles both readings. **Not reconciled by
anyone yet.**

### 12.5 Which era does discipleship open in?

- **[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §5, heading:** *"Discipleship. **Medieval.** Colonists worship the founders, partake in the rituals, and are raised as disciples."*
- **`WAYSTONE.md` §4, Industrial row:** *"Their thinnest era, because the real unlock is **disciples** — the altar opens, and a chosen few can finally be lifted."*
- **[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §3:** *"**Disciples remain the Industrial unlock**, unchanged from #10 and Waystone §4."*

So #21 reads #10 as saying Industrial, and #10's own heading says Medieval.

**A reconciliation exists and has not been ratified:** #10 §9's staging line —
*"Medieval you build a cathedral around it and it happens to your people; Industrial you
work out what it was doing the whole time and put it in a capsule"* — reads as
**psychic discipleship in the Medieval, genetic access in the Industrial.** That would
make both sources right about different things. Nobody has said so.

### 12.6 The victim of the rite — willing follower, or converted prisoner?

- **`WAYSTONE.md` §3:** the rite *"will not take a stranger. It wants **one of your own, going in willingly**, because you asked."*
- **[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §4:** *"some of them **willingly give themselves**, goes to you to fuel you."*
- **[#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §4:** the rite is a **prisoner sacrifice**, built on vanilla's `SacrificePrisoner`, whose roles are `moralist` and `prisoner`. The victim *"must be a believer — for the rite only"*, which costs conversion time.

A converted prisoner at high certainty satisfies both descriptions. **A colonist
volunteering does not fit the chosen mechanism at all**, and §10's tiers-of-godhood
ladder is paid in *willing human sacrifices*. Whether the congregation member who walks
in willingly is representable is a seam between the fiction and the mechanism, not a
disagreement about the fiction.

### 12.7 `cosmology-in-practice.md`'s standing, and the `cultivation` line

**The word is settled and the document was not corrected.**
[#21](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/21) §8 records the conflict —
`cosmology-in-practice.md` §5 bans `cultivation` by name while `CONTEXT.md` carries
**Cultivation** as canonical — and Conrad resolved it **in favour of `CONTEXT.md`**: *"I
agree, cultivation is fine."* A rename to `vigil` was proposed and rejected. **§5 should
be corrected rather than obeyed on this point, and has not been.**

**And the same ruling downgraded the whole document:** *"Treat the 'cosmology in
practice' doc as light reading, nothing more."* That sits against `CLAUDE.md` and
`COSMOLOGY.md`, which both point at it as the operative guide, and against its own
header, which says several of its rules fail silently. **Nobody has decided what its
standing actually is.** Everything cited from it in §4 and §11 above is marked
[SETTLED] on the strength of the fact it cites in `COSMOLOGY.md`, not on the document's
own authority.

### 12.8 How long is the campaign?

- **`WAYSTONE.md` §1:** *"roughly six hundred in-game days."* `VISION.md` agrees.
- **[#5](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/5) §2:** *"**There is no day budget and this ticket sets none.**"* Its reference frame is ~80–100 hours, *"~10–12 in-game years is the shape"* — 600 to 720 days — with Industrial *"plausibly four in-game years on its own."*

Compatible at the low end and not at the high end. More to the point, **one source
asserts a budget and the other declines to set one.** Soft, and it only bites when
somebody prices a pacing decision against 600.

### 12.9 Act I's beat list against Act I's schedule

`QUESTLINE.md` §1 enumerates three Neolithic beats (altar complex → capsule teaser →
first vector). [#10](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/10) §9
enumerates a different two (end of N1: the sacrifice quest opening the psychic path;
middle of N2: the genius gene). **They are probably the same story counted differently**
— the genius gene would arrive *as* the first vector — but nobody has aligned them, and
the capsule teaser has no home in #10's schedule. Owned by
[Acts I and II](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/37).

## 13. Deliberately open

Story questions nobody should close in passing. Each is open by decision, not by
neglect.

- **How many were marked** (§12.3), and whether another marked line exists.
- **The shape of the tension between the two climbers**, and the Deserters' own account of themselves. `COSMOLOGY.md` §10 holds it open; [#32](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/32) owns it.
- **Acts III–VI.** [#38](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/38) is ticketed; IV, V and VI are fog, graduating one at a time.
- **Use-based advancement** — agreed as better, deferred as too deep. Revisit after playtesting.
- **The Cult** — the congregation that worships you *off* your map: asset, liability, or a claim on you. Map fog.
- **The ideological mirror** — converting what you recruit, and holding what you cannot convert. Map fog, carrying a known trap: certainty never falls from bad mood, so any *"unhappy colony ⇒ faith collapses"* design must build that lever itself.
- **How a political chapter closes** — specifically **erasure**: what wiping a faction off the map is in play, when a settlement can only change hands and never vanish.
- **How loudly the non-era beats are announced** (First Thought, First Wall, First Ingot, First Spark). The era moments are settled: the rite *is* the event, and the world visibly ages with it.

**Tensions `WAYSTONE.md` §8 holds open on purpose** — anyone proposing a resolution
should know they are resolving something, not discovering it:

- **How much power to hand over at the start.** Too much and the founders' arc has nowhere to go; too little and the opening does not deliver on *you are gods*.
- **How far to lean on our own code.** Every custom system is a desync surface; every avoided system is a piece of the vision not delivered.
- **How early real fortification arrives.** Period-correct and satisfying, and also the thing that ends the game's difficulty.

*(§8's third tension, "whether allies can climb with you", was closed by
[#5](https://github.com/cjd721/Rimworld-Archinity/issues/5): yes — one or two per
chapter close, and the rest fade.)*
